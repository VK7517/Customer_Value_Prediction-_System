# Customer Lifetime Value Prediction

## Project Overview

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) Prediction is a Machine Learning project that predicts the future revenue a customer is expected to generate based on their past purchasing behavior.

The project uses transaction-level retail data and converts it into customer-level features such as:

- Recency
- Purchase Frequency
- Historical Revenue
- Average Order Value
- Total Quantity
- Unique Products
- Customer Tenure
- Recent Revenue
- Spending Trend
- Country

A Machine Learning regression model is used to predict future customer revenue. The trained model is integrated with a Streamlit web application so users can enter customer information and receive a CLV prediction.

## Problem Statement

Businesses have many customers, but not every customer contributes the same amount of revenue.

If a business can estimate how much revenue a customer is likely to generate in the future, it can make better decisions about:

- Customer retention
- Marketing campaigns
- Customer segmentation
- Promotional offers
- Resource allocation
- Customer targeting

The goal of this project is to build a Machine Learning system that uses historical customer purchasing behavior to predict future customer revenue.

## Project Objectives

1. Store retail transaction data using MySQL.

## Feature Engineering

The original dataset contains transaction-level records, while the prediction task is customer-level. Therefore, transaction data is aggregated to create one row per customer.

The project separates historical and future periods using a cutoff date.

```text
Historical Transactions
          |
          v
   Customer Features
          |
          v
     Cutoff Date
          |
          v
   Future Transactions
          |
          v
   Future Revenue Target
```

### Recency

Number of days since the customer's last purchase.

```text
Recency = Cutoff Date - Last Purchase Date
```

### Purchase Frequency

Number of unique invoices/orders made by the customer.

```text
Purchase Frequency = Number of Unique Invoices
```

### Historical Revenue

Total revenue generated by the customer during the historical period.

```text
Transaction Revenue = Quantity x UnitPrice
```

### Total Quantity

Total number of products purchased by the customer during the historical period.

### Unique Products

Number of different products purchased by the customer.

### Customer Tenure

Number of days between the customer's first purchase and the cutoff date.

### Average Order Value

Average revenue generated per order.

```text
AOV = Historical Revenue / Purchase Frequency
```

### Average Quantity per Order

```text
Average Quantity per Order =
Total Quantity / Purchase Frequency
```

### Orders per Month

Approximate number of orders made by the customer per month based on customer tenure.

### Revenue - Last 90 Days

Revenue generated by the customer during the last 90 days of the historical period.

### Revenue - Last 30 Days

Revenue generated by the customer during the last 30 days of the historical period.

### Previous 90-Day Revenue

Revenue generated by the customer during the 90-day period before the recent 90-day period.

### Spending Trend

Difference between recent 90-day revenue and previous 90-day revenue.

```text
Spending Trend =
Recent 90-Day Revenue - Previous 90-Day Revenue
```

A positive value indicates increased recent spending compared with the previous period.

### Country

The customer's country based on historical transaction information.

## Target Variable

The target variable is:

```text
future_revenue
```

It represents the revenue generated by a customer during the defined future period after the cutoff date.

```text

```

The input features are created from historical behavior, while `future_revenue` is calculated from the future period.

## Evaluation Metrics

### MAE — Mean Absolute Error

Measures the average absolute difference between actual and predicted future revenue.

**Lower MAE is better.**

### RMSE — Root Mean Squared Error

Measures prediction error while giving more importance to larger errors.

**Lower RMSE is better.**

### R² Score

Measures how well the model explains variation in the target variable.

**Higher R² is better.**
